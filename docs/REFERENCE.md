# Reference: drum kits, typical patterns, synth presets

Moved out of the old README in v31. The kits, patterns and presets themselves are unchanged.

## The drum kits

18 kits ship in `kits/`, all **synthesized, not sampled** -- built from
oscillators/noise/envelopes shaped to land in the character of a classic
drum machine, not a recording/rip of one. The original four:

- **`kits/808/`** -- sine kick with a pitch envelope, tone+noise
  snare, band-passed noise claps, six-square-wave metallic hats, a tunable
  perc voice. Generated by `tools/gen_808_kit.py`.
- **`kits/909/`** (default) -- punchier and more aggressive: faster pitch sweep and a
  louder click on the kick, brighter/noisier snare, tighter 4-burst clap,
  a rimshot-style perc (tone spike + noise crack). Generated by
  `tools/gen_909_kit.py`.
- **`kits/707/`** -- cleaner and tighter: short kick with minimal pitch
  sweep, sine-based "glassy" hats (instead of the 808/909's square-wave
  metallic ones), a cowbell-style perc (two closely-tuned tones beating
  against each other). Generated by `tools/gen_707_kit.py`.
- **`kits/linn/`** -- 80s-digital-drum-machine style: a very fast "thwip"
  kick (almost all its pitch drop happens in the first ~20ms), a bright/
  snappy near-gated snare, a dense 5-burst clap, glassy hats, and a woody
  sidestick-style perc. Generated by `tools/gen_linn_kit.py`.

10 more, added in a later round for more sonic variety (each generated by
its own `tools/gen_<name>_kit.py`, all sharing common synthesis building
blocks in `tools/kit_synth_common.py` rather than each re-implementing
kick/snare/clap/hat/perc synthesis from scratch):

- **`kits/606/`** -- light and papery, softer than the 808 -- thin analog
  hats, a gentle low-punch kick with almost no click.
- **`kits/cr78/`** -- an early-vintage, combo-organ-era character: warm,
  soft-edged, understated, for ballad/lo-fi/downtempo grooves.
- **`kits/acid/`** -- aggressive and driving: hard-clipped kick, snappy
  tight clap, hard hats -- built to sit under a resonant acid bassline.
- **`kits/industrial/`** -- harsh and metallic: hard-driven kick/snare, a
  metallic "clang" in place of a tuned perc voice, dense high-passed hats.
- **`kits/gabber/`** -- hardcore/gabber territory: a heavily overdriven,
  crunchy-clipped kick pushed well past clean saturation.
- **`kits/trap808/`** -- a deep, long-tailed sub kick (the trap-style "808":
  pitched and sustained, almost bass-like) paired with very short, crisp
  hats built to read clearly even in dense rolls.
- **`kits/lofi/`** -- ordinary synthesized drum voices run through
  deliberate digital degradation (`bitcrush()`: amplitude quantization +
  sample-and-hold downsampling) for a dusty, filtered lo-fi-hip-hop texture
  -- no vinyl-rip samples involved, just processed synthesis.
- **`kits/dub/`** -- deep and spacious via long natural decays (this engine
  has no convolution reverb, so the "room" comes from a long-ringing open
  hat/perc and roomy noise tails) -- pair with the FX tab's reverb send.
- **`kits/minimal/`** -- ultra-short and clicky: every voice here decays
  much faster than any other kit, for minimal techno's negative-space
  aesthetic.
- **`kits/funk/`** -- warm and full-bodied, a little more "live" than the
  808/909 kits, with a woody conga-like perc voice -- funk/disco/boom-bap.

Switch kits live from **Menu tab > KIT** or the **PERFORM tab's KIT row**
(both browse/apply the same selection -- no relaunch needed either way), or
pass `--kit kits/<name>` on the command line. All 14 are verified via
`t8_selftest` (NaN/silence/clipping/mute-solo/step-timing checks) exactly
like the default kit, plus a dedicated load-count check confirming all 6
required samples load cleanly for every one of them.

**Adding your own sounds:** drop WAV files into a folder under `kits/` using
these exact names: `kick.wav`, `snare.wav`, `clap.wav`, `chh.wav`, `ohh.wav`,
`perc.wav`. Point the app at it with `--kit kits/yourkit` (edit
`mux_launch.sh` on the device, or pass the flag when running locally). Any
sample rate/bit depth works; it's resampled to the engine's rate on load.

**Extra takes per pad:** add `kick_2.wav`, `kick_3.wav` (up to `_8`) etc.
alongside the primary file and the engine picks them up as alternate
variants for that pad -- select which one a given step plays with **X + Y**
on the Sequencer tab (cursor on that step first). A small number badge
shows on any step using a non-default variant.

## Typical drum patterns

The PERFORM tab's **TYPICAL PATTERN** row (see "Control scheme" above) drops
a genre-shaped groove into all 6 drum tracks, built from well-established
rhythmic conventions of each style rather than random placement. These are
step-position conventions -- decades-old, commonly-taught patterns -- not a
transcription of any specific copyrighted recording; the **AMEN** template
in particular is deliberately "amen-*style*", not a literal transcription of
the original 4-bar performance (which has real bar-to-bar variation and
microtiming that, per every reference consulted while building this, can't
be reduced to one clean 16-step grid anyway -- same spirit as the
synthesized kits being "808-style"/"909-style" rather than samples). Source
code + reasoning: `src/core/DrumPatterns.cpp`.

- **FOUR ON THE FLOOR** -- the foundational disco/house/techno pulse: kick
  on every quarter note, clap doubling beats 2 and 4, straight 8th-note
  closed hats, one open-hat accent at the end of the bar. No swing.
- **HOUSE** -- same 4/4 kick, but the hats move to the classic house "chick"
  placement (open hat on the off-beat 8ths, closed hat only on the beat)
  plus a light shuffle and a couple of shaker-style perc accents.
- **TECHNO** -- relentless straight kick, dense driving 16th-note closed
  hats with off-beat open-hat accents, sparse clap, syncopated perc clicks.
  No swing -- techno reads as mechanical.
- **ELECTRO** -- 80s electro-funk/Miami-bass style: a syncopated, bouncy
  kick (deliberately *not* four-on-the-floor) mirrored each half-bar, a
  clap backbeat (the signature electro snare sound), tight straight 8th
  hats, cowbell/perc-style syncopated accents. No swing -- the bounce comes
  from the kick syncopation itself, not shuffle.
- **BREAKS** (breakbeat) -- funk-break-style: syncopated kick, snare
  backbeat with a ghost hit before the loop restarts, straight 8th hats
  with a 16th pickup, a bit of swing since breaks are rooted in played funk
  drumming rather than a machine grid.
- **AMEN** (amen-style, see caveat above) -- captures the break's most
  recognizable trait: a syncopated/"pushed" snare against a kick that lands
  early too, over straight 8th-note hats. A simplified one-bar loop.
- **HIP HOP** (boom bap) -- laid-back 90s-style kick/snare backbeat with a
  noticeably wider swing than the other templates -- boom bap's "MPC feel"
  comes largely from shuffle, which is the one thing a grid-based sequencer
  can approximate (real records also use deliberately off-grid timing that
  swing alone won't fully capture).

20 more were added in a later round after specific feedback asking for more
variety in house/techno/electro/breaks (researched via web search across
several production guides/tutorials for each family) plus a handful of new
genre families:

- **House family**: **DEEP HOUSE** (sparser off-beat-only hats, more
  swing), **TECH HOUSE** (house's kick + techno's dense hats + a syncopated
  percussion loop), **UK GARAGE** (kick pulled off the straight 4/4 grid
  entirely, heavy shuffle -- 2-step's defining "broken" feel).
- **Techno family**: **MINIMAL TECHNO** (stripped to just kick + sparse
  clicks, no snare/clap/open-hat at all), **ACID TECHNO** (driving 4/4 with
  hard hats, room left for a resonant bassline), **DUB TECHNO** (kick on 1
  and 3 only, clap only on 2/4 per Basic Channel-style convention, minimal
  hats), **INDUSTRIAL TECHNO** (kick AND hat on every single 16th, metallic
  clang perc accents), **EBM** (pounding kick on every 8th note for a
  militaristic pulse).
- **Electro family**: **ELECTRO FUNK** (a second, busier syncopation
  pattern than the original ELECTRO template).
- **Breaks family**: **BREAKBEAT** (UK big beat/nu skool breaks -- a snare
  backbeat added on top of a syncopated kick), **JUNGLE** (a faster,
  busier amen-style chop with extra ghost snares, played unswung like a
  time-stretched break), **DRUM AND BASS** (a cleaner, more "programmed"
  2-step pattern), **DUBSTEP** (true half-time: kick on 1, snare on 3 --
  confirmed via research into the genre's defining "cut time" groove --
  sparse hats leave room for a wobble bassline).
- **Hip-hop family**: **BOOM BAP** (a second classic-90s take distinct from
  the original HIP HOP template), **TRAP** (syncopated kick, clap on beat 3
  half-time, a dense closed-hat bed standing in for the genre's
  mixed-resolution hi-hat rolls -- for an actual roll, try the ratchet
  combo on a hat step), **LO-FI HIP HOP** (sparse, heaviest swing of the
  set, no accented kick at all).
- **A few more**: **DISCO** (four-on-the-floor + the classic open-hat
  "chick" on every off-beat 8th), **FUNK** (syncopated 16th-note kick
  placement -- the syncopation itself IS the funk), **AFROBEAT** (a
  simplified stylization of the genre's dense, interlocking percussion
  bed), **REGGAETON** (a simplified "boom-ch-boom-chick" dembow-riddim
  stylization). AFROBEAT and REGGAETON in particular have real-world
  rhythms with more cross-rhythm/micro-variation than one fixed 16-step
  loop can capture -- same "genre-style, not a literal transcription"
  honesty as AMEN above applies to those two especially.

Each template also carries a suggested `pattern.swing` value, applied
alongside the steps when you press A. **RANDOM (ANY)**, the default browse
position on that row, picks a different template at random each press --
browse to a specific name with Left/Right if you want that one on purpose.
Every template tiles cleanly across pattern lengths above 16 steps and
truncates cleanly below it.

## Synth presets

> **v44:** the library below (16 presets) was replaced by 56 presets in 6 categories (BASS, LEAD, PAD, KEYS, PLUCK, FX), chosen in the large preset browser; see `CHANGELOG.md` (v44)
> and `src/core/SynthPresets.cpp`. `t8_presetaudit` prints the level / spectrum / layers of every preset. The text below describes the original 16 and is kept as design background.

The Synth tab's **PRESET** mode (X+Y, see "Control scheme" above) drops a
complete patch -- every `SynthPatch` parameter, including which engine it
uses (Subtractive, FM, or Wavetable) and the automation LFO -- onto
whichever synth (1/2/3) you're currently editing, switching that synth's
engine type too if the preset uses a different one from what's currently
loaded. There are 16 built-in presets: 10 for the original Subtractive
engine, mapped from genuinely researched sound-design conventions for each
style (not guessed) and adapted honestly to what that engine actually is --
**one monophonic oscillator per voice, one filter, one free-running LFO**,
not a multi-oscillator/unison synth -- plus 3 for the Phase 5 FM engine,
built from well-established FM sound-design conventions (inharmonic
operator ratios for bells, a bright high-ratio modulator for electric-piano
attack transients, sub-harmonic modulator stacking for bass growl), plus 3
for the Phase 5b Wavetable engine, demonstrating POSITION-scanning across a
couple of the 4 built-in banks. Source code + per-preset reasoning:
`src/core/SynthPresets.cpp`.

- **ACID BASS** -- TB-303-style squelch: sawtooth into a low, high-
  resonance filter with a short envelope decay so the classic opening
  "pew" sweep happens right at the front of every note, plus a bit of
  glide for slides between notes.
- **TECHNO STAB** -- square wave, punchy filter-envelope hit, no sustain
  (self-contained stab per note, not a held tone) -- the classic
  Drumcode-style techno stab shape.
- **WARM PAD** -- multi-second attack/decay/release, saw wave, a gentle
  slow (0.12Hz) low-depth LFO breathing on the filter cutoff for movement
  without being distracting.
- **WOBBLE BASS** -- square wave with a triangle LFO patched hard into the
  filter cutoff at an eighth-note-ish rate -- the dubstep wobble technique,
  one held note per bar doing all its movement via the LFO.
- **REESE BASS** -- an honest *approximation*, not a literal reproduction:
  the real technique detunes two sawtooths against each other; this
  single-oscillator engine stands that in with a slow, very shallow
  pitch-LFO for a similar "moving" texture, over a dark, long-sustained
  low end.
- **SUB BASS** -- pure sine wave, filtered clean, zero resonance, zero
  envelope/LFO movement -- a sub's whole job is to sit still under the
  mix.
- **PLUCK** -- near-zero attack, very short decay, no sustain, a filter
  that snaps briefly open right on the attack then closes almost as fast.
- **BRASS STAB** -- sawtooth with a genuinely slow (800ms) filter-envelope
  bloom and a ~100ms amp attack for that characteristic brass swell-in,
  plus a subtle 5.5Hz vibrato.
- **TRANCE GATE** -- the classic "trance gate" IS rhythmic on/off volume
  gating: a bright saw held with a long sustain and a fast, square-shaped
  LFO on volume that snaps between near-silent and full level every cycle
  (an actual gate, not a smooth tremolo).
- **AMBIENT DRONE** -- WARM PAD's slower, darker cousin: near-maximum
  attack/release so notes fade in/out over ~2 seconds, softer triangle
  wave, and a much slower (0.08Hz), deeper filter LFO sweep for a texture
  that keeps visibly evolving rather than just gently breathing.

And the 3 FM presets (Phase 5, `src/core/FMSynth.h`/`.cpp` for the engine
itself):

- **FM BELL** -- serial-chain algorithm (op0 carrier modulated by op1,
  modulated by op2, modulated by op3) with steeply climbing, INHARMONIC
  operator ratios (1 -> 3.5 -> 7 -> 11) -- that inharmonicity is what reads
  as metallic/bell-like rather than a plucked string. Zero sustain, long
  release: every note rings and decays on its own, never held open.
- **FM EPIANO** -- the classic DX7 electric-piano idea in miniature: a 1:1
  carrier/modulator pair for the fundamental "tine" tone, plus a 14:1 ratio
  operator (a textbook FM e-piano modulator ratio) at low depth for the
  bright metallic attack transient. This engine's single shared envelope
  can't give that transient its own independent fast decay the way a real
  multi-envelope DX7 patch would -- an honest simplification -- so the
  whole voice instead uses a moderate decay/short sustain/short release to
  read as "struck and fading".
- **FM BASS** -- one carrier (ratio 1) fed by 3 parallel modulators (a
  sub-harmonic at ratio 0.5, plus ratio-1 and ratio-2 modulators at modest
  depth) -- parallel sub-harmonic/harmonic stacking is a well-established
  FM bass-growl technique, adding low-end harmonic content and grit without
  the pitch instability a detuned-oscillator approach risks that low.

And the 3 Wavetable presets (Phase 5b, `src/core/Wavetables.h/.cpp` +
`src/core/WavetableSynth.h/.cpp` for the engine itself):

- **WT SWEEP PAD** -- the CLASSIC bank (sine -> tri -> saw -> square) with a
  slow (0.1Hz) LFO targeting **WT POS** instead of the usual cutoff/pitch,
  so the timbre itself morphs continuously over several seconds -- a long
  attack/release keeps it firmly in pad territory.
- **WT PWM LEAD** -- the PULSE WIDTH bank with a much faster (3.5Hz) WT-POS
  LFO sweeping duty cycle back and forth, a bright open filter, and a
  short, punchy amp envelope -- the classic pulse-width-modulation lead
  texture, done here by scanning a wavetable instead of the usual
  dual-oscillator PWM trick.
- **WT VOWEL BASS** -- the FORMANT bank held at a fixed low POSITION (no
  LFO movement), dark filter, long sustain -- a static "ah"-ish vowel color
  sitting under a bassline rather than an evolving lead texture.

Save your own on top of these: dial in a patch on the Synth tab (PARAMS
mode), switch to PRESET mode (X+Y), press **B**, and type a name on the
typed name -- it's written to `presets/<name>.t8syn` and shows up
in the PRESET browse list immediately (alongside the 16 built-in ones),
no restart needed. Same on-disk-vs-compiled-in split as custom drum
patterns vs. the built-in genre templates above.


## Timing: swing, nudge, ghost (v39)

Every step has the same length. Swing delays the hit on every odd step by swing / 2 of a step (pattern swing 0-75 %, or a track's own
SWING in the bottom row of the track panel; PAT = follow the pattern). NUDGE (right-click a step) moves one hit by -50 ... +50 % of a
step; negative values work on drum tracks only. GHOST plays the step at velocity 0.45. The order inside a step is: swing offset +
nudge, and ratchets spread from there to the next step line. Saved as `nudge`, `ghost` (trailing step fields) and `trackSwing`.

## Reverb and delay (v38)

**Reverb** (FX tab, REVERB): SIZE is the decay time (0.5 about 0.3 s, 0.8 about 0.9 s, 0.9 about 2 s, 0.97 six seconds and more), DAMP
makes the tail darker (0 = bright, 1 = highs die 8 times faster than the lows), PRE is the pre-delay in milliseconds, LEVEL the return
level. The tail is stereo: send a mono track and it still comes out wide. Per-track REVERB SEND values are unchanged.
**Delay** (DELAY): the division follows the tempo; when you change it the repeats glide to the new time (a short tape-style bend)
instead of clicking. FEEDBACK sets the number of repeats, TONE how fast they get dark (0 dark, 1 bright), PING-PONG alternates
the repeats left and right, LEVEL is the return level.

## The v37 synth filter

`SynthFilter.h` has three modes, chosen with the FILTER row of a subtractive patch: **12 DB** (state-variable, -12 dB/oct),
**24 DB** (four-pole ladder, -24 dB/oct, loses bass as the resonance rises, like a Moog) and **ACID** (the same ladder voiced for
303 lines: -18 to -21 dB/oct, earlier saturation, less make-up gain). The oscillators and the filter run at 96 kHz and are
decimated by a half-band filter. RESONANCE above about 95 % makes the filter self-oscillate on the cutoff; use a short ENV AMT
sweep and the KEYTRACK knob to play it as a sine voice. In ACID mode an accented step (velocity 1.0) opens the filter further,
consecutive accents more so, and a slide takes 60 ms (or GLIDE if that is longer) and is linear in pitch. DRIFT adds a slow analog
wander of pitch and cutoff. The wavetable engine uses the 12 dB mode of the same filter.

## The v35 kits (24-bit / 48 kHz, natural levels)

Generated by `t8_kitgen` (`tools/kitgen_main.cpp`): every sound is built at 192 kHz and converted with the engine's own
sinc resampler. Kicks and snares are centred mono files, hats / claps / shakers are stereo (L/R correlation about 0.5-0.65, so the
mono sum stays solid). Peak levels keep drum-machine proportions: kick -0.8 dBFS, snare -2.5, clap -3.5, open hat -6.5,
closed hat -9. Every kit has 2-3 takes of kick, snare and perc (`kick_2.wav` ...), 2 closed hats and 1-2 claps.

- **`kits/electro/`** -- punchy 808/909 hybrid for Rother-style electro: fast-sweep kick (50 Hz), tonal snare, 4-burst clap,
  metallic 909-style hats, conga / cowbell / rim as perc.
- **`kits/techno/`** -- a longer, driven kick (47 Hz, long tail, `kick_2` for rumble basses), short dry snare, wide clap, tight hats.
- **`kits/breaks/`** -- acoustic-like breakbeat drums: short beater kick, cracking snare with a room tail (`snare_3` is the long one),
  noise hats, shaker / bongo / rim.
- **`kits/acid_v2/`** -- hard-driven 909-leaning kit with a 54 Hz kick, 909 hats and clap, 808 cowbell and tom as perc.
  (The older `kits/acid/` stays.)

Your own samples: drop a `.wav` on the window with a drum track selected. The current kit is copied into `kits/mykit` (then
`mykit_2` ...), the slot gets your file (any rate / bit depth, converted on load), and the project switches to that kit.
